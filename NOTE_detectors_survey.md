## The one column that matters, and how to read it

A four-bucket phase-shifting read is photon-limited at full well: photons per second
= pixels × full well × frame rate.  The deck's interferometer scaling (2.3 pm at
1e14 photons on the 30 nm surface, lens rig; 1/√N) turns that into picometers per
one-second reading.  The "pm per 1 s" column below is that conversion — the
photon-limited precision of a 1 Hz COPHI reading on each camera, before any bench
error, on OUR reconstruction (every pupil pixel used).  Two facts the table makes
plain:

1. **Well depth × pixel rate decides, not pixel count.**  A line-rate-limited sCMOS
   keeps about the same photons per second at any ROI height (pco.edge 5.5: 8.3e12 at
   2560 × 2160 / 50 fps and 7.8e12 at 2560 × 128 / 789 fps), so on that architecture
   a smaller format buys CADENCE, not precision per second.  The deep-well
   Photonfocus 1024² (200 ke) and the fast charge-domain Gpixel GSPRINT parts are the
   way to more photons per second; EMCCDs are readout-limited (iXon 897 at unity gain,
   2.6e12).
2. **Feng's baseline camera (1k², 60 ke, 30 Hz) is the slow end of the table:**
   17 pm per 1 s reading.  The best streamable true-GS cameras found reach 2–4 pm per
   second; bursts to RAM 1.8 pm but not continuously.

## The survey as delivered

Scope rule applied: only cameras whose exposure is simultaneous for all pixels are counted as candidates — true CMOS global shutter (charge-domain or pipelined), CCD frame-transfer, EMCCD frame-transfer. Rolling-shutter sCMOS are listed in a separate "excluded / flagged" group because several of them advertise a "global" mode that is **global-reset + rolling readout** or **"effective global shutter" by trigger timing** (exposure window restricted to the overlap of all rows); those do not give a true snapshot and are flagged as such.

**Photon budget** = pixels × full well (e⁻) × max frame rate at that format, in the mode that gives the quoted full well, assuming every pixel is driven to full well every frame (upper bound on photons/s available to a shot-noise-limited phase estimate). Where a vendor gives frame rate only at 8/10-bit, that rate is used and noted. "n/s" = not stated on the cited page.

QE at 532/633 nm: almost no vendor states these two wavelengths explicitly; where only a peak or a curve is given, that is what is written (with wavelength of the peak). Treat the column as "not stated at 532/633" unless a number is given.

### What is realistic — per class (the agent's reading)

**1k class, now.** For a 12–16-bit, continuously streamed, *true snapshot* 1k×1k-ish frame at ≥30 fps the realistic choices are (a) the two CIS2521-based sCMOS in GS mode — **pco.edge 5.5** (50 fps full, 105 fps at 1280×1024, 30 ke⁻, 2.5 e⁻ rms; budget ~8e12 e⁻/s) and **Andor Zyla 5.5** (49/52 fps, same sensor) — with the caveat that GS halves the rate and roughly doubles read noise versus their RS mode; (b) the **Photonfocus MV1-D1024E-160** (true 1024×1024, 10.6 µm, 150 fps, **200 ke⁻**, 220 e⁻ read noise) which, because of its enormous well, delivers the largest photon budget of the ≤1k-pixel streaming set (3.1e13 e⁻/s) — for a PSI whose precision is photon-limited rather than read-noise-limited this matters more than low read noise; (c) **Sony IMX421/IMX422** machine-vision cameras (~2 MP at 4.5 µm, ~24–26 ke⁻, 5–6 e⁻, 130–240 fps in commercial cameras, up to 370–430 fps at the sensor) — cheap, 12-bit, true Pregius GS, HW-triggered per frame; the FLIR BFS-U3-20S4M (131 fps) gives 6.5e12 e⁻/s and a CXP camera on the same sensor ~2e13. The Pregius S 4th-gen parts (IMX537 etc.) trade well depth (≈9.5–9.8 ke⁻) for pixel count and are no better per second. For *burst* work, the **pco.dimax S4** (1008×1008, 36 ke⁻, 4,467 fps to RAM) and **Phantom VEO 1310** (1280×960, 11.7 ke⁻, 10,860 fps to RAM) give ~1.6e14 e⁻/s but cannot stream that rate. The **Gpixel GSPRINT4502** (2048×1216, >30 ke⁻, 850 fps 12-bit) and the Basler boost GSPRINT4521 (31.6 ke⁻, 230 fps at 21 MP) are the highest *streamable* global-shutter budgets found (6e13–1.5e14 e⁻/s) but need CXP-12 frame grabbers. None of the Hamamatsu ORCA, Kinetix, Prime, Marana, pco.edge 4.2/panda or GSENSE-based sCMOS cameras qualify: every one is rolling shutter; their "global reset", "global exposure timing", "Global Clear" and "Effective Global Shutter" modes are trigger/overlap tricks, not snapshot exposure.

**1k class, 2026–27.** Sony's stacked-BSI Pregius S trio (IMX947 5120² @278 fps 12-bit, mono samples April 2026; IMX949 4096×3072 @502 fps 12-bit; IMX927 105 MP @100 fps) will put high-rate true-GS cameras into Basler/FLIR/LUCID bodies in 2027, but Sony's own flyers list full well as "TBD" so the photon budget cannot be computed yet. Gpixel's **GSPRINT2001BSI** (1280×1024, 20 µm BSI GS, 12 ke⁻, 19 e⁻, 6,400 fps, sampling Dec 2025) is the first BSI global-shutter part in the 1k-fast class; no camera built on it was found. Teledyne e2v Emerald Gen2 is in production but 2.8 µm pixels (small wells, FW not stated).

**512 class.** The frame-transfer EMCCDs (iXon Ultra 897: 512², 180 ke⁻, 56 fps; 888: 512² crop 93 fps) are true snapshot devices and useful if run at low/unity EM gain (budget ~2–2.6e12 e⁻/s, limited by the slow 17–30 MHz serial readout, not the well). The sCMOS GS cameras at 512-row ROI (pco.edge 5.5: 208 fps) deliver 3–4× more photons per second than the EMCCDs at this format. Pregius IMX421/422 at 512×512 ROI would run faster than full frame but the vendors do not publish the ROI rate — ask. SPAD arrays (SPAD512S) are a non-starter for this use: 8-bit at 400 fps is ~3e10 counts/s, three orders below CMOS.

**256 and smaller.** This is the AO-WFS niche: **OCAM²K** (240², 2,067 fps; 120² binned 3,700 fps; 92 % QE at 650 nm; 43 µs latency) and **Nüvü HNü 128 AO** (128², 500–1000 fps). Both are EMCCDs engineered for photon-starved operation; for a bright heterodyne fringe they would be run at unity gain and the useful budget is set by the register/ADC, not the 270 ke⁻ pixel well — the 3.2e13 figure above is a pixel-well upper bound. The machine-vision high-speed CMOS cameras (Mikrotron EoSens 1.1/2.0CXP2 at 128×128: 18–24 kfps; sub-LVDS GSPRINT parts) are faster still but their well depths are not published, so the budget cannot be computed from vendor data. A pco.edge 5.5 at 2560×128 GS (789 fps) keeps ~7.8e12 e⁻/s — the line-rate-limited sCMOS budget is roughly constant across ROI height, which is the key fact for the speed-vs-resolution trade on that architecture.
